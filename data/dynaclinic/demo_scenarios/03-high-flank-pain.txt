Mock case: 48-year-old man on phenelzine with severe flank pain

A 48-year-old man with treatment-resistant major depressive disorder and hypertension presents with sudden, severe left flank pain radiating to the groin, with nausea. Medications are phenelzine 15 mg three times daily and amlodipine. He has no allergies. Temperature is 37.0 C, heart rate 96/min, and blood pressure 146/88 mm Hg. Urinalysis shows microscopic hematuria without pyuria. Serum creatinine is 0.9 mg/dL. Non-contrast CT of the abdomen and pelvis shows a 4-mm stone in the left distal ureter with mild hydronephrosis.
