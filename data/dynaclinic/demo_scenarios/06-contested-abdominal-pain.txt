Mock case: 81-year-old man with severe abdominal pain and kidney disease

A 81-year-old man with chronic kidney disease (eGFR 22 mL/min/1.73m2), atrial fibrillation not on anticoagulation, and heart failure presents with 5 hours of severe periumbilical abdominal pain out of proportion to a soft, mildly tender abdomen. Lactate is 4.1 mmol/L. Heart rate 118/min irregular, blood pressure 98/60 mm Hg. Plain abdominal radiograph is unremarkable. He takes furosemide and metoprolol.

Age: 81
Sex: male
History: chronic kidney disease stage 4; atrial fibrillation; heart failure
Medications: furosemide; metoprolol
Allergies: none
Labs: lactate 4.1 mmol/L; eGFR 22 mL/min/1.73m2
Imaging: Plain abdominal radiograph: unremarkable

Question: Which of the following is the most appropriate next step in management?
A. CT angiography of the abdomen now, with intravenous fluids
B. Non-contrast CT of the abdomen to avoid contrast nephropathy
C. Abdominal ultrasound and serial lactate in 6 hours
D. Start oral ibuprofen and observe
E. Colonoscopy within 24 hours
