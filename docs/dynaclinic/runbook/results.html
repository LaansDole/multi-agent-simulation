DynaClinic: benchmark results

Does dynamic multi-agent switching beat one agent? Three arms on the same cases and model: single (one Primary Care Agent), dynamic (Meta-Controller triage, care team only when needed, Clinical Critic only when divergence exceeds tau 0.25) and static (the full care team plus Critic on every case). All runs are real, on OpenAI gpt-6-luna, 2026-10-27.

Bottom line

gpt-6-luna is close to its ceiling on both benchmarks, so neither one separates single-agent from multi-agent yet. With the proposal's three modes (since f15) the adversarial debate runs on a third of MedQA, but accuracy stays within the intervals and the dynamic arm now uses more calls than the old always-on team (6.94 vs 6.00 per MedQA question). Before f14, dynamic switching matched the full team with about 36% fewer calls. With a weaker model (qwen3.5-4b) there is headroom, but single (68%) still leads dynamic (60%) and static (62%).

Update 2026-10-28: uncertainty score and the proposal's three modes (dynamic arm)

Since f14 and f15 the Meta-Controller computes a Complexity Score C and an Uncertainty Score U (normalised entropy of a generalist's top-k differential) and picks the mode with one rule: U above tau_u is adversarial (Proponent, Clinical Critic as Opponent, Mediator); otherwise C at most 0.5 is hierarchical (Primary Care hub with up to 2 consults); otherwise collaborative (care team plus one mesh exchange round). tau_u is 0.15 for multiple choice and 0.59 for open-ended input, each the 75th percentile on these same vignettes: calibrated, not validated. Only the dynamic arm changed (single and static keep identical prompts), so only it was re-run.

What MedQA shows

  1. No accuracy benefit from the multi-agent arms. The intervals overlap almost completely, and the single agent is one question ahead.
  2. Shared misses point at the model, not the orchestration. Three questions were missed by all three arms with the same wrong answer.
  3. The team once talked itself out of the right answer. On 0243, both team arms answered C where the single agent was right (A).
  4. The adversarial path never ran. Specialists almost always agreed (mean divergence 0.010), so the switch to the Clinical Critic, the core of the proposal, was not tested.
  5. The safety numbers mean little here. MedQA questions have no structured patient data and no marked unsafe options, so the safety gates had almost nothing to check.

Next step options

  • A larger or harder sample: more MedQA questions, or only the harder Step 2&3 questions, to narrow the intervals.
  • A weaker base model, so there is headroom for the team to help.
  • Safety-focused cases with structured patient data and marked unsafe options, so the safety gates and the adversarial path actually get exercised.
